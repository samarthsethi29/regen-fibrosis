 

Human Repair-Outcome Axis — Work Plan v2 

Sep 27, 2026 · @Naveen Kumar 

This plan rebuilds, from raw files, a human Repair-Outcome Axis — the transcriptional programme that separates scar-free from scarring repair — and tests it with five pre-specified hypotheses. It integrates 31 bulk datasets (about 1,360 samples), 12 single-cell datasets (about 180 samples, about 900,000 cells) and a degree-controlled graph-learning benchmark, all analysed in R over about 22 weeks, for a journal with an impact factor of about 3. 

Introduction 

Human tissues can heal with or without a scar, yet no study has asked what the scar-free cases have in common at the molecular level. This study answers that across every public human repair model with suitable data. 

Background 

Scarring is common and costly. Each year in the developed world about 100 million people acquire scars after 55 million elective and 25 million trauma operations, and an estimated 11 million live with keloids (Bayat et al., BMJ 2003). 

Scarring is one end of a wider fibrosis spectrum. Nearly 45% of deaths in the developed world are attributed to chronic fibroproliferative disease (Wynn, J Pathol 2008). 

Some human repair is scar-free. Fetal skin regenerates until about 22 weeks of gestation, with less inflammatory infiltration, distinct fibroblast behaviour and a different TGF-β isoform balance (Textbook on Scar Management, NCBI Bookshelf). 

Oral mucosa heals faster and with less scarring than skin. Wound-activated transcriptional networks are already present in unwounded oral mucosa, driven partly by SOX2 and PITX1 in keratinocytes (Iglesias-Bartolome et al., Sci Transl Med 2018). 

Adult skin can repair without scar after fractional laser injury, giving a third, within-person scar-free model (GSE168760). 

Pathological scars have distinct cell states. Keloids carry an expanded mesenchymal fibroblast subpopulation that is also increased in scleroderma (Deng et al., Nat Commun 2021). 

Research gap 

One context at a time. Published transcriptomic studies compare keloid with skin, or oral mucosa with skin, but never test whether one programme separates outcome across contexts. 

Three signals are mixed. Differences between biopsies reflect injury magnitude, which cells the biopsy contains, and cell-intrinsic state. Existing signatures do not separate them. 

A collagen-centred view. Fibrosis signatures are dominated by ECM genes, but our pre-analysis suggests the shared outcome difference lies in inflammatory amplitude and a non-ECM residual. 

Graph learning has not been applied to repair outcome. Graph convolutional networks integrating omics with interaction networks found 165 new cancer genes (Schulte-Sasse et al., Nat Mach Intell 2021), but such models reward highly connected genes and are easily evaluated with leakage. 

Rationale 

Contrasting scarring with scar-free repair in several independent human models cancels context-specific noise, so what is shared is more likely causal for outcome. Single-cell references then separate composition from cell state, and graph learning extends the evidence to genes that bulk data measure poorly. A sealed cohort of keloid-prone individuals tests whether the axis predicts outcome in people it has never seen. 

Novelty 

The study compares repair outcomes, not disease versus normal tissue, and separates three sources of signal that earlier fibrosis signatures mix together. 

Outcome contrast across three human regeneration models. Scar-free repair (oral mucosa, fractional-laser adult skin, fetal skin) is contrasted with scarring repair (adult scars, hypertrophic scars, keloids) in the same analysis. 

Injury, composition and cell state are separated. Acute-wound reference datasets remove injury magnitude. Single-cell deconvolution removes differences in which cells a biopsy contains. What remains is cell-intrinsic. 

A shared axis that is not collagen-centred. The pre-analysis found that immune amplitude and a non-ECM residual are shared, while ECM genes add nothing. v2 tests this formally. 

Graph learning that cannot cheat. Networks are built without expression or text-mining evidence, so they are independent of the labels. Models are tested on held-out genes in held-out contexts, stratified by node degree, against rewired-network nulls. 

A locked, pre-registered test. The keloid-prone cohort GSE113619 has never been analysed. It is scored once, with a one-sided test fixed before reprocessing. 

Significance 

Clinical. Genes on the axis that are not injury genes are candidate targets for scar prevention. A sample-level axis score could flag keloid-prone healing early. Transfer to SSc and Asherman syndrome would show relevance beyond skin. 

Methodological. The injury–composition–cell-state decomposition and the degree-controlled graph benchmark are reusable for any multi-context disease meta-analysis. 

Resource. Harmonised, raw-file-derived objects for 43 human repair datasets will be released with code. 

Objectives 

Primary objective 

Define a cross-context human Repair-Outcome Axis that separates scarring from scar-free repair, and test whether it predicts keloid-prone repair in a sealed cohort. 

Primary discovery endpoint: concordance of regenerative and fibrotic contrasts (H1). 

Primary validation endpoint: the pre-registered interaction test in GSE113619 (H5). 

Secondary objectives 

Decompose the axis into injury magnitude, tissue composition and cell-intrinsic parts (H2). 

Localise axis genes to cell types and states: keloid vs normal scar in three datasets, fetal vs adult skin, SSc, Asherman and the wound time course (H3). 

Benchmark graph learning against non-graph and propagation baselines under degree control, and prioritise network-supported candidate genes (H4). 

Transfer and specificity: test the axis in SSc and Asherman cohorts, and check that chronic and acute wounds score no higher than their injury level. 

Mechanism: infer upstream transcription-factor activity and ligand–receptor changes along the axis. 

Exploratory objectives 

Timing: trace programmes across oral (6 h to day 7), laser (day 3 to 21) and wound (day 1 to 30) time courses. 

Interventions: test whether EGCG (two trials) or CD133+ cell therapy for Asherman syndrome (GSE311899) moves tissue along the axis. 

Spatial: map the axis onto Visium sections of wounds, keloids and SSc skin. 

In vitro persistence: test which axis parts persist in cultured fibroblasts (six datasets).

Study design 

A multi-context meta-analysis of public human data, with a sealed test cohort and pre-specified roles for every dataset. Nothing about a dataset’s role can change after P0. 

Definitions 

Context: one repair setting with a defined outcome. Regenerative: R1 oral mucosa, R2 fractional-laser adult skin, R3 fetal skin. Fibrotic: F1 adult scar formation, F2 hypertrophic scar, F3 keloid. 

Contrast: one within-dataset comparison, always signed so that positive = towards scarring. For regenerative contexts it is the scarring response minus the scar-free response (an interaction, or a difference of paired responses). 

Axis: the gene-level, context-weighted meta-analytic effect across training contrasts. 

Injury response: wound vs intact skin in reference arms that are neither scar-free nor pathological.

Blinding and leakage rules 

GSE113619 is downloaded and checksummed in P1 but not opened until P9. The axis gene set is frozen and its hash recorded first. 

A dataset that serves as an injury reference is never adjusted against itself (leave-dataset-out references). 

Graph-learning labels are recomputed for every held-out context; node features never use contrast results. 

Workflow 

Twelve phases run in five streams over about 22 weeks. Bulk and single-cell processing run in parallel, and every phase ends at a gate with a pre-specified fallback (see Risks).

Known traps, handled by rule in Phase 2: 

GSE28914: rebuild from CEL (processed values are on mixed scales). 

GSE209609: custom ENSG_at probe IDs; map through Ensembl, not the platform table. 

GSE124161: NCBI counts are broken (median 4 reads per library); use the submitter read-count file. 

GSE113619: NCBI counts missing; submitter file has Entrez IDs in an unnamed column; sum technical replicates; libraries are shallow (0.12–0.68 M reads), so score at sample level only. 

GSE152781: take treatment from the EGCG/placebo field; the untreated day-0 biopsy is the uninjured baseline. 

GSE58095: group by the disease field, not by autoantibody. 

Phase-by-phase plan 

Each phase is one R script run in RStudio. It writes its tables (xlsx), figures (PDF + 300-dpi TIFF) and a log, and ends with a printed gate decision. 

P0 Set-up and pre-registration (week 1) 

New project folder, renv lockfile, fixed seed 2026. 

config.yaml: dataset registry, contrast definitions, thresholds and gates from this plan. 

Freeze this plan as a timestamped release (GitHub tag or OSF) before any download. GSE113619 stays sealed. 

Gate: registry validates against GEO/ArrayExpress (all accessions resolve). 

P1 Raw-file download (weeks 1–2) 

Bulk: raw files by the rules above; single-cell: per-sample matrices and author metadata only (about 10 GB). 

Manifest with URL, size, MD5, date; resumable downloads. 

Gate: 100% of core files present and checksummed; any missing file triggers the fallback source (NCBI or submitter counts). 

P2 Bulk preprocessing and QC (weeks 2–4) 

Platform-specific normalisation (table above); one gene-symbol space. 

Sample QC: RLE/NUSE for Affymetrix, inter-array correlation (outlier if z < −3), PCA. 

Sex check (XIST vs RPS4Y1, DDX3Y, KDM5D) against metadata to catch label swaps. 

Positive controls: injury markers (KRT16, KRT6A, S100A8, MMP1) up in wounds; fibrosis markers (COL1A1, COL3A1, POSTN, COMP) up in HTS and keloid. 

Freeze one object per dataset (expression, counts, curated sample sheet, QC flags). 

Gate: ≥ 90% of samples pass; positive controls in the expected direction in ≥ 80% of fibrotic datasets. Failures follow pre-specified rules. 

P3 Within-dataset contrasts (week 5) 

limma-voom with TMM (counts) or limma-trend (arrays); duplicateCorrelation for repeated subjects; interaction models for R1, R2 and the locked test. 

Standardised effect d = coefficient / √s² (posterior), with its standard error; π₁ per contrast; per-timepoint contrasts kept for trajectories. 

Gate: ≥ 12,000 genes measured in ≥ 70% of training contrasts. 

P4 Repair-Outcome Axis (weeks 6–7) 

Regenerative contrasts are signed so that positive = scarring response minus scar-free response. 

Two-stage random-effects meta-analysis per gene: within context, then across contexts (REML with Hartung–Knapp; DerSimonian–Laird as sensitivity). 

Concordance of regenerative and fibrotic contrasts with a pairing-aware sample-label permutation (1,000); leave-one-context-out stability. 

Programme decomposition: camera tests of 22 GO programmes and 12 cell-type marker sets; GO:BP GSEA; concordance after removing immune, injury, ECM and epidermal genes vs random removal. 

Injury-adjusted axis as pre-specified sensitivity. 

Outputs: Figures 2–3; axis table (gene, estimate, SE, FDR, heterogeneity, sign consistency). 

Gate (H1): see Aims. 

P5 Single-cell processing (weeks 3–9, in parallel) 

Import per sample; emptyDrops for unfiltered matrices; per-sample QC by median absolute deviation (UMIs, genes, % mitochondrial); scDblFinder doublets. 

Clustering per dataset after Harmony by sample; cell typing into 14 lineages by marker-module scores, checked against author labels (GSE241132, GSE163973, GSE265972, E-MTAB-8142). 

Donor × cell-type pseudobulk (≥ 20 cells); limma-voom contrasts with donor blocking and chemistry covariates. 

Axis score per cell type; fetal vs adult fibroblasts, keratinocytes and myeloid cells. 

Outputs: Figure 4. 

Gate (H3): ≥ 80% agreement with author labels, then H3 as in Aims. 

P6 Composition decomposition (weeks 10–11) 

Reference profiles: adult skin (E-MTAB-8142, healthy GSE138669, GSE191067, intact GSE241132), oral mucosa (GSE164241), endometrium (GSE215968 controls). 

Deconvolve every bulk sample (InstaPrism, a fast BayesPrism; NNLS on marker genes as fallback). Validate on simulated pseudobulk (Pearson r ≥ 0.8 per major lineage). 

Refit contrasts with the main cell fractions as covariates to get the composition-adjusted axis. 

Split each gene’s effect into composition, injury and residual parts. 

Gate (H2): see Aims. Decides whether the epidermis and fatty-acid programmes are reported as biology or as composition. 

P7 Networks and null models (weeks 11–12) 

STRING v12, experiments and curated-database channels only (combined score ≥ 0.7); co-expression and text-mining channels excluded to avoid circularity. 

Healthy-skin co-expression network from single-cell metacells (reference donors not used in any contrast), top 10 neighbours per gene. 

100 degree-preserving rewired copies of each network. 

Gate: axis genes closer in the network than in rewired copies (proximity z > 2). If not, the graph model is still run but reported as a negative benchmark. 

P8 Graph learning benchmark (weeks 12–15) 

Design in the next section. 

Outputs: Figure 5; ranked gene list with attributions. 

Gate (H4): see Aims. 

P9 Validation and locked test (weeks 16–17) 

Locked GSE113619: axis score per sample (singscore on the top 100 scarring and top 100 scar-free genes, fixed in P4), then the pre-registered interaction test. Opened once.

Statistical analysis plan 

All tests, thresholds and multiplicity rules below are fixed in P0. Anything added later is labelled post hoc. 

Effect sizes 

Each gene g in each contrast gets a standardised effect from limma, so arrays and RNA-seq are on one scale: 

d_g = \frac{\hat\beta_g}{\sqrt{\tilde s^2_g}}, \qquad \mathrm{SE}(d_g) = u_g 

Here the estimated coefficient is divided by the moderated (posterior) residual standard deviation, and u is the unscaled standard deviation of the coefficient. Regenerative contrasts use the interaction form: 

\Delta_g = (\text{skin}_{wound} - \text{skin}_{base}) - (\text{scar-free}_{wound} - \text{scar-free}_{base}) 

Axis meta-analysis 

Two-stage random effects: contrasts within a context are pooled first, then contexts are pooled, so large datasets cannot dominate. Each stage uses inverse-variance weights with between-study variance: 

\hat\theta = \frac{\sum_i w_i d_i}{\sum_i w_i}, \qquad w_i = \frac{1}{u_i^2 + \hat\tau^2} 

The between-study variance is estimated by REML with the Hartung–Knapp correction (DerSimonian–Laird as sensitivity). A gene enters the axis if measured in ≥ 3 contexts. Heterogeneity is reported as I², τ² and sign consistency. 

Concordance and permutation 

The H1 statistic is the mean Spearman correlation between the regenerative composite and each fibrotic contrast. Its null comes from 1,000 pairing-aware sample-label permutations (labels swapped within subjects that have both groups, shuffled across the rest): 

p = \frac{1 + \#\{\bar\rho^{*} \ge \bar\rho\}}{B + 1}, \qquad B = 1000 

Multiplicity 

Primary endpoints: fixed sequence. H1 is tested at α = 0.05; the locked test (H5) is tested at α = 0.05 only if H1 passes, so no α splitting is needed. 

Secondary hypotheses (H2–H4): Holm correction across the three. 

Gene level: Benjamini–Hochberg FDR < 0.05 within each analysis. 

Enrichment: FDR < 0.05 within each gene-set collection. 

Models and covariates 

Repeated biopsies: donor as a blocking factor (duplicateCorrelation) or paired design. 

Known batches and chemistry as fixed covariates; sex where recorded and balanced. 

Single-cell: donor-level pseudobulk only (never cells as replicates); a cell type is tested only with ≥ 20 cells in ≥ 3 donors per group. 

Keloid results are pooled across the three datasets by random-effects meta-analysis. 

Power 

Locked test: before opening GSE113619, simulate its power from the variance of sample-level scores in comparable wound datasets and record it in the frozen plan. 

Single-cell: 3 vs 3 donors detects only large effects, so claims rest on replication across datasets, not single p-values. 

Sensitivity analyses (pre-specified) 

Leave each dataset out; DerSimonian–Laird instead of REML; arrays only vs RNA-seq only; injury-adjusted axis; flipped GSE151464 included; sorted-cell datasets removed; phase-aligned oral contrasts (skin D7 vs palate D3); alternative gene-set sizes for the locked-test score (50, 100, 200). 

Graph-learning design 

The model must predict how genes it has never been told about behave in a context it has never seen. It gets credit only for what the network adds beyond gene features and node degree. 

Task. Node regression: predict each gene’s axis effect (primary); classify consensus scarring vs scar-free genes (secondary). 

Splits (leakage-proof). Leave one context out × 5 gene folds. For held-out context c, labels are recomputed from the other contexts only. Test genes have their labels hidden. Predictions are scored against the observed effects in context c for the test genes. 

Inputs. Graphs from P7 (STRING curated + healthy-skin co-expression, as separate and combined graphs). Node features that never use the contrasts: cell-type expression profiles from healthy single-cell references (skin, oral, endometrium; about 40 values), GO-slim membership, transcription-factor, ligand and receptor flags. Training-gene labels are passed as an input channel (label trick) so propagation is possible.

Metrics. Spearman ρ between predicted and observed held-out effects; AUPRC for the top decile. Every metric is reported within node-degree quartiles, because hub genes inflate naive scores. 

Implementation. R torch (CPU), with GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT and GIN layers written as sparse matrix operations. Hyperparameters by inner cross-validation; 5 seeds per setting; early stopping on validation genes. 

Output. A final ensemble trained on all contexts ranks every gene. Network-supported candidates are genes the model ranks highly that are not significant in the meta-analysis alone. Ethics, data availability and reproducibility 

Ethics. Only public, de-identified data collected under the original studies’ consent and approvals are used; no new human material is collected. Confirm with the institutional ethics office whether a formal exemption letter is needed. Controlled-access data are not used, and no re-identification is attempted. 

Attribution. Every dataset is cited by accession and original publication. 

Data availability. Processed objects, the axis table, the frozen gene set and the ranked gene list are deposited on Zenodo; all accessions are listed in the supplement. 

Code availability. One script per phase on GitHub, archived with a Zenodo DOI at submission. 

Reproducibility. renv lockfile, fixed seeds, config.yaml, download manifests with MD5, sessionInfo per phase, and the frozen plan tagged before download. 

Reporting. Dataset selection follows a PRISMA-style flow; the locked-test analysis is reported against its pre-registered specification.
